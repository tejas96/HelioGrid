# SCR-M01-05 · Business Profile

Single write-point for logo, tax registration, address, bank details; skippable onboarding step and later prompt-point.

**Module:** M01 · **Personas:** EPC Owner · **Context of use:** appears **three times** in the product's life — as a skippable step during company onboarding (laptop-leaning, mobile-capable — M01 §2); as the in-context prompt-point fired when the first proposal is about to be sent (M01-24, M01-29 context: "configure in context, not in a settings maze"); and as the settings screen for revisiting (M01-29: "Settings screens exist for revisiting, not for setup"), which owner ruling `Q75` made load-bearing by putting the tenant's segment and typical-size declarations here. *(This line read "twice" and named only the first two until 2026-08-28; Entry & exit named three all along.)* Permission: `F2.M01.manage-tenant-settings` (EPC Owner); settings changes are audit events (M01 §M01.3 permissions).

## Entry & exit

Reached from: the onboarding sequence after "What do you sell?" (M01 §M01.3 behavior detail); later, the prompt-point when the first proposal is about to be sent — prompt-points offer *do it now inline* or *keep skipping* (M01 §M01.3 behavior detail); and settings for revisiting ("Settings screens exist for revisiting, not for setup" — M01-29 context). Leads to: on skip during onboarding, the next onboarding step (the skippable invite step, then the two-door landing — M01 §M01.3 behavior detail); on save, back to wherever the prompt fired. Consumers (proposal, agent script, customer link, invoice) read this one profile by reference — they never re-ask (M01-31). **This screen is also where the tenant's segment and typical-system-size declarations are edited after onboarding (owner ruling 2026-08-28, `Q75`).** `M01-23` collects them once on `SCR-M01-04` and stores them; it named no later surface, so that screen's *change this later* line and its failed-save escape had nothing to point at. They sit here for the reason the rest of this profile does — one write-point the consumers read by reference and never re-ask (`M01-31`). The size is declared in **`kWp`** (`Q74`), tiered `estimated · Declared by you`, and changing it re-seeds only what comes next: a sent proposal keeps its prices. Their permission is this screen's own — `F2.M01.manage-tenant-settings`, and the change is an audit event like every other settings change here.

**Further decisions made in design (2026-08-28) — later screens inherit them.**

1. **Five collapsible sections in place, never five rows that each open a sheet.** Three shapes were possible for eleven fields. *All of it at once* is the whole control surface (`F7-34` forbids it); *five rows opening sheets* is exactly the settings maze `M01-29` exists to replace — the sheet grammar (`F7-21`) is for editing something whose context is elsewhere, and here the profile **is** the screen. Collapsible sections, one open: the reader sees all five parts and where each stands, and meets one part's controls.
2. **One forward act, and no separate Skip.** With nothing filled in, *skip* and *save* are the same write — two controls would be two labels for one act, and a skip that discarded typed input would be a destructive control on an onboarding step (`N8`). The act stays live with nothing answered (`M06-22` killed the Next-disabled rule).
3. **The two declarations are present in the onboarding appearance too, as a read-back.** `Q75` puts them here for editing *after* onboarding, and the literal reading would hide the part during the step — but `SCR-M01-04`'s line *you can change both in your company profile, the next step* is a promise made one tap earlier. The part stands **Answered**; nothing is re-asked.
4. **The prompt-point is this screen's tax-registration field set inside `EditorSurface`, over the proposal it is holding up.** One field set, two containers — which is why the parts are authored as field sets rather than page sections, and why `F7-21`'s sheet-on-mobile / panel-on-desktop switch is performed by the system rather than restated here.
5. **The part titles are the profile's parts, not the brief's field list** — *Logo and company name · Address · Tax registration · Bank details · What you sell*. The first joins two facts because they are one act: the two things a document shows at the top.
6. **A logo's limits are stated before the upload, not only inside the refusal**, and the refusal names **every** limit missed — a file can be small enough and still too low-resolution, and a refusal mentioning only size sends the owner back with the wrong fix.
7. **A limit is not a value and carries no tier.** A size ceiling, a minimum edge, a format's character count — a tier says how a value was arrived at, and a limit was not arrived at, it was set. Recurs on every screen that states a constraint.
8. **Density follows the life, not the screen (`F7-17`, owner ruling 2026-08-28).** The onboarding and prompt appearances are **expressive** — onboarding and authentication are on that list. The **settings appearance is `functional`**, because `F7-17` names *settings and administration* on the functional list and the method is to pick the mode from what the screen **is**. The corridor argument that carries the onboarding appearance does not reach the settings one: it sits in no corridor, it is one destination among many. `SCR-SHELL-03` used the same method to rule its inbox *not* settings; this life is. Spacing and radius tighten on those two frames only — the 44px floor is the components' and does not move, and capability is untouched.
9. **The settings appearance reuses `SCR-SHELL-01`'s shell exactly** — four destinations, `Home · Leads · Quotes · More`, with the raised centre action; settings is reached from **More**, never as a rail entry. Three things change with the chrome and nothing else: the wordmark goes, the corridor's act becomes this screen's own `Save changes`, and the centred two-column composition goes at 1536 because a settings screen sits in the app's grid. **Capability is identical across all three lives.**


## Requirements (verbatim)

- **M01-24** (P0) — **The company profile is skippable and prompted in context.** Logo, tax registration, address, bank details are a skippable onboarding step — prompted later, **when the first proposal is about to be sent** (the moment they are actually needed). - **M01-31** (P0) — **One Business profile screen feeds many places.** Company name, logo, address and tax registration are asked once and used by the proposal, the agent's script, the customer link and the invoice — never asked twice by different surfaces. _(non-UI half, build-side: single write-point for identity facts; proposal, agent, link, invoice consume by reference — for awareness, not for drawing)_

## States

Base: **loading** · **empty** · **error**. **`empty` resolves to the default frame and gets no separate drawing (settled 2026-08-28).** A profile with *nothing known* cannot occur: `M01-01` captures company name, owner name and city at signup and admits no other tenant-creating path — `M01-08` sends a known phone to login on the existing account, and `M01-09`'s request-to-join lands inside a tenant that already has a profile. So the emptiest this screen ever is, is the profile with nothing **the owner has added yet** — no logo, no tax registration, no bank details — with the name and city read back from signup, and that is the default. Same resolution `SCR-M01-03` and `SCR-M01-04` reached, argued from this screen's own facts.

Screen-specific:

- **skipped** — the whole step skipped during onboarding; tax registrations stay empty until the first proposal (M01-24); a tenant with no config at all breaks nothing (M01 §M01.3 context, M01-28 — not a row of this slice).
- **tax-format-invalid-explained** — a malformed registration is explained against the market's format (from `pack.tax`), never silently rejected, never a hard wall; skip stays available (M01-25).
- **prompt-at-first-proposal-send** — the later prompt-point fires when the first proposal is about to be sent, inline, offering do-it-now or keep-skipping (M01-24; M01 §M01.3 acceptance).
- **normal** — the profile filled and saved: logo, the market's tax registration(s), address, bank details, and the two declarations `Q75` placed here. The main frame of the screen, and the one the other states are departures from. *(Added 2026-08-28 — this list named four edge states and no default; every neighbouring brief names one.)*
- **logo-invalid-limits-stated** — a logo uploaded at the profile step is invalid → validated on upload with the actual limits stated (M01 §M01.3 edge list, the `S6B.wrong.5` pattern).
- **declarations-edited-reseed-scope** — the segment and typical system size (`kWp`) edited after onboarding, with the scope of the change stated on the surface: it re-seeds only what comes next, and **a sent proposal keeps its prices** (`Q75`, `M01-23`; the price-book law of `F1`). The size carries its tier — `estimated · Declared by you` — wherever it renders, exactly as on `SCR-M01-04`. *(Added 2026-08-28 with the `Q75` ruling, which reached Entry & exit and not this list.)*
- **arrived-from-a-failed-save** — reached by `SCR-M01-04`'s *Finish later* route, where a save of those two declarations failed. The screen says what is still undeclared rather than presenting an empty field as a choice, so the owner does not have to remember what the previous screen lost. *(Added 2026-08-28: `Q75` made this an entry point, and an entry with no state is how a route arrives somewhere that does not expect it.)*

## Data volume

One company-profile record: one logo, the market's tax-registration types (from `pack.tax` — the set and formats are market-pack data; the module never names one market's tax ID in UI copy, M01 §M01.3 localization notes), one address, one set of bank details.

## Numbers carrying provenance

Every rendered **quantity** carries its F8 provenance tier in the design. **The two values this screen collects are identifiers, not quantities, so neither carries a tier (owner ruling 2026-08-28, `Q76`):** the tax registration and the bank details are tenant-entered identity facts consumed by reference everywhere (`M01-31`). Applying a tier to one would be nonsense — `estimated` on a registration number claims a guess nobody made, `measured` claims a verification nobody performed. **What each one does owe is a plain statement of what was checked**, beside the value, which is `F8-03`'s own mechanism: `M01-25`'s live check tests the **market pack's format** and nothing else, so the screen says the format was checked and the issuing authority was not. Never a fifth tier and never a parallel standing — `F8-03` forbids both by name. The one true quantity this screen holds is the typical system size (`kWp`, `Q75`), which keeps the tier it was declared with on `SCR-M01-04`: `estimated · Declared by you`. A logo's stated limits are rules, not values, and carry nothing. No computed money or date renders here.
